Bonus lesson

The Mirror Modifier

Many things you model are symmetrical: a mug body, a face, a house. With the Mirror modifier you build only one half and Blender creates the other half for you, updating it live as you edit. The manual says it mirrors the mesh along its local X, Y and/or Z axes, across the Object Origin.

1. Add the modifier

Open the Modifiers tab in the Properties editor, as in Lesson 7, and add Mirror from the Generate group. Under Axis choose the axis to mirror along. The manual explains that mirroring on X turns the positive X values of the original into negative X values on the other side.

2. Useful options

Clipping stops vertices from moving through the mirror plane while you edit, so the two halves stay joined. Merge joins a vertex with its mirror copy when they are within the Merge Distance. Bisect cuts a mesh that already sits on both sides of the plane and keeps one side to mirror. If you pick more than one axis you get more copies: two axes give four, and three axes give eight.

3. Place the mirror plane exactly

The mirror plane passes through the object's origin, so the origin has to be on the middle line of your model. The manual describes a method: select the edge, snap the 3D Cursor to the selection, then use Set Origin and choose Origin to 3D Cursor. That uses the two bonus lessons on the 3D Cursor and the Origin. An empty used as the Mirror Object is the other option the manual mentions.

When you finish modeling, you can click Apply to make the mirrored mesh real, or leave the modifier in place to keep editing. Read the manual page on the Mirror modifier.

Exercise

Add a cube, enter Edit Mode, and delete the vertices on its left half. Leave Edit Mode, add a Mirror modifier on the X axis, and watch the cube come back. Then turn on Clipping and try dragging a vertex on the middle line.
