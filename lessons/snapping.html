Bonus lesson

Snapping

When you move something with G, it follows the mouse freely. Snapping makes it jump to exact points instead, so objects line up without guessing. Do not confuse it with the Shift+S Snap menu from the last lesson: that one moves things once. Snapping works while you are moving.

1. Turn snapping on

Click the snap On/Off button in the header of the 3D Viewport, or press Shift + Tab. The manual also says you can hold Ctrl while moving to snap only for that move.

2. Choose what to snap to

Open the Snapping popover. The manual lists these Snap Target choices:

Increment moves in steps the size of the grid cells. Grid snaps to the grid shown in the viewport. Vertex snaps to the vertex closest to the mouse cursor. Edge snaps to the closest edge. Face snaps to the surface of faces in mesh objects.

3. Choose the Snap Base

Snap Base decides which point of your selection does the snapping. The manual lists Active (the origin of the active object in Object Mode), Median (the median of the selection), Center (the current transformation center) and Closest (the vertex closest to the target). With Closest you can pick a corner of one cube and drop it on a corner of another.

Read the manual page on Snapping.

Exercise

Add two cubes. Turn on snapping, set Snap Target to Vertex, select the second cube and press G. Move the mouse near a corner of the first cube and click. Turn snapping off with Shift + Tab when you finish.
